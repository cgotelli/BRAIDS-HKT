# Remaining G4 water AMR work

This audit was performed from the G4 overlay and its inherited GeoClaw/AMRClaw sources before changing the AMR path. Fixed-grid qualification does not establish the AMR behavior described below. Subsequent [isolated AMR accounting development](../validation/hydraulics/amr/README.md) adds read-only observations and native static-lake/wet-pulse checks; it does not yet correct or qualify the open wet/dry transfer and inlet requirements.

The smallest useful extension is **two-level interior refinement with every physical boundary retained at level 1**. Refining an inlet introduces a separate space/time ownership problem and should follow that first qualification.

## Current execution path and barriers

| Component | Actual behavior and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Inlet preparation | [braids_boundary_prepare](../validation/hydraulics/channels/native/braids_boundary_module.f90#L412) rejects `level != 1` or `mxnest != 1`. It gathers one adjacent cell per fixed `(side,index)` key and checks the declared base spacing/bed. [Ghost and flux application](../validation/hydraulics/channels/native/braids_boundary_module.f90#L1329) also convert coordinates with the base-grid spacing. Removing the guard would misidentify fine faces. |
| Positivity | [braids_positivity_prepare](../validation/hydraulics/channels/native/braids_positivity_module.f90#L83) requires complete, unique global cell coverage on one level. Its rectangular arrays cannot represent a partial fine level or exclude covered coarse cells. [The draining kernel](../validation/hydraulics/channels/native/braids_draining_module.f90#L10) treats domain-edge fluxes as fixed and shares each interior correction between two equal-resolution cells; a coarse/fine interface has neither ownership pattern. |
| Accepted observations | [braids_flux_begin](../validation/hydraulics/channels/native/braids_flux_module.f90#L274) requires exactly one complete level and one owner for every external face. Volumes are sums of that level's patch interiors. Summing levels would double-count covered coarse water. |
| Step lifecycle | [tick](../validation/hydraulics/channels/native/tick.f#L400) starts observations, arms inlet fluxes, prepares positivity, advances, and commits all three before fine subcycling and [update](../validation/hydraulics/channels/native/tick.f#L549). The present ledger therefore cannot observe subsequent restriction or coarse/fine flux correction. |
| Existing AMR machinery | [prepare_advanc](../validation/hydraulics/channels/native/advanc.f#L33) fills ghosts and saves coarse states. [par_advanc](../validation/hydraulics/channels/native/advanc.f#L428) calls `qad`, advances the patch, then accumulates `fluxsv`/`fluxad` registers. `tick` already partitions fine timesteps to reach parent time and invokes synchronization. These hooks exist; their presence does not qualify the new inlet or limiter on them. |

The inherited files cited below were checked byte-for-byte against the preserved G3 build. The [Makefile](../validation/hydraulics/channels/native/Makefile#L39) selects shallow-water `update`, `upbnd`, `filval`, `filpatch`, and `qad`, together with AMRClaw flux-register and regridding routines. The isolated [G4 build map](../tools/g4_hydraulics.py#L24) currently permits only `advanc.f`, `tick.f`, and `stepgrid.f` as shared-library overlays; additional replacements need explicit target registration.

## Conservation and positivity issues that must be resolved

1. **Synchronization can change water after a positive patch update.** Inherited [upbnd](../vendor/avac-qgis/avac-main/clawpack-v5.14.0/geoclaw/src/2d/shallow/upbnd.f#L105) skips a flux-register correction when the coarse cell is dry, and then zeros corrected depths below `dry_tolerance`. Both discarded corrections and this reset need explicit mass accounting. A positive coarse update and positive fine updates do not guarantee a positive coarse cell after reflux. Clipping that result would not meet the water-budget gate.
2. **Water transfer differs from the AVAC depth-transfer option.** [update](../vendor/avac-qgis/avac-main/clawpack-v5.14.0/geoclaw/src/2d/shallow/update.f90#L148) uses wet-cell surface/depth averages in water mode; the alternative averages conserved depth over all children. [filval](../vendor/avac-qgis/avac-main/clawpack-v5.14.0/geoclaw/src/2d/shallow/filval.f90#L190) reconstructs surface elevation and clips against fine bed in water mode. The [default is `conserve_depth_amr=.false.`](../vendor/avac-qgis/avac-main/clawpack-v5.14.0/geoclaw/src/2d/shallow/geoclaw_module.f90#L55); only AVAC's setter enables the alternative. Enabling that option alone is not a water fix: direct depth reconstruction over varying bed can disturb a constant water surface. Wet/dry prolongation, restriction, represented bed and lake-at-rest balance need a joint design and tests.
3. **The limiter must share a space/time transfer budget.** Fine substeps can draw from a neighboring coarse cell whose provisional update has already advanced. Independently limiting each level does not bound the later register correction or give one donor budget. Define a common coarse/fine transfer, constrain its accumulated outgoing volume, and apply the same correction to both representations. Momentum corrections must retain the pressure/topography balance used by the f-wave method.
4. **The register path includes a Riemann correction.** [qad](../vendor/avac-qgis/avac-main/clawpack-v5.14.0/geoclaw/src/2d/shallow/qad.f#L60) contains an old comment about skipping the routine, but its `return` is commented out. The active calls add coarse/fine Riemann fluctuations to the register. Any limiter/register integration must account for that term, not assume `fluxad` and `fluxsv` alone describe the final correction.
5. **Regridding changes both state and ownership.** [regrid](../vendor/avac-qgis/avac-main/clawpack-v5.14.0/amrclaw/src/2d/regrid.f#L70) invokes grid replacement and rebuilds flux registers and boundary lists. [gfixup](../vendor/avac-qgis/avac-main/clawpack-v5.14.0/amrclaw/src/2d/gfixup.f#L135) fills new grids and frees old storage. Cached patch IDs, donor maps and face owners need an explicit hierarchy generation; no cache may survive a topology change merely because its numeric patch ID is reused.

## Minimal extension route

- [ ] Add read-only composite-volume and transfer observations first: count uncovered coarse cells plus fine cells, and record water immediately before/after coarse/fine correction, restriction, new-grid interpolation and grid deletion. Retain per-level substep records; commit the composite water budget only at synchronized times. Separate physical-boundary delivery from internal transfers.
- [ ] Introduce an explicit two-level, static **interior-only** layout. Keep a level-1 collar wide enough to exclude refined ghost/interpolation stencils from physical boundaries, and reject any refined inlet callback. Prepare and commit inlet Q/history only for its owning level-1 interval; fine substeps must neither repeat that Q nor update its accepted-stage anchor.
- [ ] Generalize cell/face ownership and the positivity budget for that layout. Start with synchronized timesteps to isolate the spatial interface, then exercise genuine 2:1 temporal subcycling and CFL retry. A partial fine-level edge is an internal coarse/fine interface, not a physical wall or freely supplied ghost donor.
- [ ] Replace or constrain the water-mode transfer/reflux operations so that positivity and conservative water transfer survive synchronization and regridding. Preserve the existing zero-correction path where it already satisfies those requirements. Add counters before accepting any discarded correction or depth reset.
- [ ] Qualify static and moving refinement using lake at rest over varying bed, a wet pulse crossing the interface in both directions, a wet/dry front crossing it, and closed-reservoir inflow/shutdown. Include repeated refine/coarsen at zero Q, restart during active Q, checkpoint/regrid synchronization, and one-versus-four-thread runs. Use the existing G4 conservation/accuracy limits; declare comparison grids and tolerances before executing new cases.

Likely native changes are `tick.f` (substep versus synchronization lifecycle), `advanc.f`/`stepgrid.f` (accepted corrected flux and phase hooks), the three `braids_*` boundary/positivity/flux modules, `braids_draining_module.f90`, and new isolated overlays for `update.f90`/`upbnd.f` plus whichever `filval.f90`/`filpatch.f90` transfers the selected design changes. `qad.f`, `fluxsv.f`, `fluxad.f`, `saveqc.f`, and regrid/grid-list construction require interface review and targeted instrumentation; they should not all be rewritten by default. Export/configuration, build registration, the independent ledger, branch-history reader, and composite profile/storage metrics must evolve alongside those native changes.

## Later: refinement on a physical inlet

Retain the section's stable string ID, but replace a single fixed face index with a unique physical interval owned by the finest active face at each accepted time. A covered coarse face must not add another copy of its discharge. New native face beds/lengths must come from the refined represented geometry, and all active pieces of an ID must participate in its global stage/flux solve.

Mixed boundary levels also evolve at different times. A level-local solve imposing the full section Q independently on each level would duplicate delivery; solving only one subset would generally break the intended global allocation. First define a synchronized physical-boundary time slab, state interpolation, per-ID volume allocation across its face pieces, and exactly when continuation history becomes accepted. A useful intermediate restriction is uniform refinement level along each complete inlet, with independently checked subcycle-integrated Q. General mixed-level sections need the more general scheduler/ownership contract.

AMR remains open until the relevant route has executable evidence. Interior refinement with level-1 boundary faces must be named as that limited scope and must not be reported as qualification of refined inlets.
