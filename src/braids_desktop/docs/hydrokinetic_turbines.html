1. What the extension computes

BRAIDS solves water depth and the two depth-averaged horizontal velocity components. Each operating turbine adds a momentum-removal source to those shallow-water equations. It slows the flow through the rotor region and allows the surrounding water to respond. The rotor is permeable: it is not a solid wall and particles can pass through it.

Power is a separate calculation using the turbine's performance data and an estimate of the incident velocity. This distinction matters because the velocity resolved by a depth-averaged mesh is not a fully resolved three-dimensional rotor inflow. The turbine also slows the water sampled near itself, so an induction correction is used to infer an incoming speed. Applying a measured power curve directly to the already slowed local speed would use the wrong reference.

For an aligned rotor with uniform incoming speed, the familiar expressions are:

A = πD² / 4
T = ½ρ A Ct U²
P = ½ρ A Cp U³

Here D is rotor diameter, A is swept area and U is incoming speed. The current extension uses water density ρ = 1000 kg/m³. Ct controls loading on the water; Cp controls useful mechanical shaft power. They are not interchangeable. Even when power is the main objective, thrust must remain credible because it influences the wake and the inflow reaching downstream machines.

Water loading and incoming velocity

For a fixed rotor, n is the unit vector along its axis and t is perpendicular to n in the horizontal plane. The solver resolves un = u·n and ut = u·t. The force acts opposite the normal component, not simply opposite the full flow vector. Its spatial support is the retained smooth Thetis actuator distribution.

α = [1 + √(1 − π D Ct / (4H))] / 2
f / ρ = −b A Ct |un| un n / (2α²)
U = √[(un/α)² + ut²]

H is local depth and b distributes the actuator over mesh cells. For speed-dependent Ct, the incoming-speed relation is solved consistently with the curve. The tangential velocity is retained in the incoming-speed estimate. This is a depth-averaged actuator approximation, not a resolved blade model.

Why there are still two main power choices

Resolved flow (Thetis) estimates power from the resolved flow and induction correction, without an analytical wake reconstruction. Dou wake correction retains the water response and additionally reconstructs the incoming wake profile for downstream rotor-power integration. Dou supplies a yaw-dependent horizontal wake shape; BRAIDS completes it vertically with the retained Gaussian assumption and uses device-specific yaw-performance data.

Equality of the fixed-axis and flow-following force at true zero local yaw does not make these whole models interchangeable. It requires the same support, coefficient convention and incoming state. A fixed turbine will acquire yaw when the water turns; a flow-following turbine represents an ideal machine that turns to stay aligned. Likewise, Dou's zero-yaw analytical wake reproduces the retained aligned wake correction, not necessarily the power obtained without any analytical correction.

The old aligned-only analytical option is now under Show legacy power option. It stays visible when an existing project uses it, to avoid changing saved results silently. For new analytical yaw/array work use Dou with the required data. Thetis remains useful as the resolved-flow alternative and comparator; it is not a special “non-yawed turbine” that must be selected whenever the mounting angle is zero.

2. A mounting angle is an input; operating yaw is an output

The former “Rotor yaw” input was misleading. It is now called Axis offset from reference. You specify where a fixed rotor points. BRAIDS then computes the misalignment between that fixed axis and the water facing it. Setting that offset to zero does not force the operating yaw to zero.

Reference direction: the downstream direction drawn with the second placement click. It provides an initial orientation and a straight-reach direction for wake screening and mesh refinement. Axis offset: the fixed counterclockwise rotation of the rotor axis from that reference. The axis is normal to the rotor plane, not along the line representing the rotor.

For example, with an eastward reference and a +30° offset the rotor axis points 30° counterclockwise from east. If the resolved local water direction is 10°, the reported resolved yaw is approximately +20°. If the water direction changes, the reported yaw changes automatically while the rotor remains fixed.

The exported local_yaw_deg uses the sampled resolved normal/tangential components and reports rotor direction minus local flow direction. The analytical wake/device calculation uses the induction-corrected incident angle. These angles need not be numerically identical because induction changes the normal speed; the sign convention inside the analytical profile also follows its own transverse coordinate. Do not use the mounting offset as a replacement for either calculated angle.

Choose Follows the flow (self-aligning) only when that describes the intended machine or modelling assumption. It removes the need to prescribe a fixed mounting axis, but does not simulate a yaw motor, controller delay or rotation limits. Making every fixed rotor automatically point into the flow would change the physical problem rather than merely calculating yaw.

3. Upstream turbines and overlapping wakes are detected automatically

You do not enter “has an upstream turbine”, row numbers or source/receiver links. At evaluation, BRAIDS checks active upstream sources, their relative positions and the direction of flow along the supplied reference. The Dou profile's displacement, width and receiver radius determine whether a wake can affect a receiver. A finite four-width envelope screens negligible tails; it is a numerical screening rule, not a sharp physical wake boundary.

The calculation then integrates the incoming velocity and device power response over the receiver disk. In schematic form:

Ureceiver(y,z) = Ureference − Σ ΔUupstream(y,z)
P = ½ρ A Σ wj Cp,0(Uj) Ryaw(Uj,γj) Uj³

The weights w sum to one over the circular rotor. The reference is obtained from the contributing first-row turbines, not arbitrarily reset at each row. Each upstream source contributes once. Its own loading and incoming state affect its contribution. This supports tandem turbines, staggered receivers and third/fourth rows without manually connecting them. Stopped turbines do not contribute an operating wake.

Supply a sensible downstream reference. Current analytical propagation is based on the placed reach direction, with flow-dependent sign and yaw-dependent wake displacement. It is not a streamline-following wake model that bends freely around river meanders. The code rejects material turning, inconsistent first-row speeds, blocked paths and other unsupported states rather than guessing. Relevant checks include a 10° flow/reference-direction limit and 10% ambient-speed consistency. Place references along a locally straight reach, not along the yawed rotor axis.

The mesh-refinement length, default 10D, does not stop a physical wake at 10D. It only controls the fine-mesh corridor. Wake detection and downstream power remain governed by the analytical model beyond that distance.

The analytical correction is not subtracted a second time from the simulated water velocity. The SWE already contains all turbine momentum sources. The analytical model addresses receiver power sampling; it does not create a separate hub-height water solution.

4. What each parameter is for

Placement tab: geometry and operation

ParameterReason and how to choose it
Turbine name and centreThe name identifies logs/CSV rows. The centre locates the rotor. Place it within the fluid domain with enough clearance and usable surrounding mesh.
Reference directionSecond placement click, pointing downstream. Used to orient the initial rotor, the local wake-refinement corridor and the straight analytical wake direction. It is not a prescribed water velocity.
Rotor orientation / axis offsetChoose a fixed mounting or ideal self-alignment. The fixed offset is measured counterclockwise from the reference. Actual flow-relative yaw is calculated, not prescribed by this field.
Diameter DControls swept area, loading/power scale, wake distances and local mesh size. Use the real rotor diameter, not the length of the placement gesture.
Hub height above bedSets the initial hub elevation relative to the prepared reference bed. For a rotor radius D/2, hub height must leave enough space below and above the disk. It is not an instruction for the rotor to move with bed changes.
Minimum bed clearanceRequired separation between rotor bottom and bed. On a flat reference bed, available bottom clearance is hub height − D/2. Local geometry checks can be more restrictive.
Minimum surface clearanceRequired water cover above rotor top. In a flat section the minimum depth is hub height + D/2 + surface clearance. Insufficient cover switches operation off; it can restart when covered.

Device tab: performance data, not wake fitting

ParameterReason and how to choose it
Performance inputConstant Ct/Cp is a diagnostic or narrowly supported operating approximation. Separate incoming-speed Ct and Cp curves are preferable for varying flow. A shared table is another input format. The stock mixed-reference Thetis table is retained for compatibility and is not admissible for fixed-axis loading.
CtDetermines turbine force and therefore wake generation. For the Dou fixed-axis route, supply zero-yaw data versus incoming speed magnitude for the actual controller. Do not apply an extra yaw correction to the input curve.
Mechanical CpConverts incoming kinetic-energy flux to shaft power. Use measured or justified device values. Existing constant-input Cp = 0 is a sentinel selecting ideal actuator-disk Cp, not a command to stop the turbine.
Speed knots and curve sourceUse speed in m/s and dimensionless coefficients. Separate curves may have different knots; Cp must cover the required operating and receiver-power range. Give rotor/controller/RPM and provenance. Unsupported high speeds are not extrapolated.
Device yaw-performance JSONContains rotor identity/diameter, fixed RPM, speed and absolute-yaw axes, P(yaw)/P(0) ratios, provenance and physical scope. Dou supplies the wake, not every rotor's yaw-power response. The table must match the zero-yaw curves; it is saved inside the project.
RPM / TSRThese define the physical operating point behind performance data. The present imported yaw table is fixed-RPM; it does not add a general variable-RPM or TSR controller. TSR = rotational angular speed × radius / incoming speed. Do not transfer a fixed-RPM curve to another controller silently.

The current UI uses one shared yaw table for fixed-axis turbines in the project. Its rotor diameter and controller must match those turbines. The supplied 1.2 m / 51 RPM demonstration table is not a universal turbine model. Its tabulated range is not equivalent to experimental validation at every entry. Existing Thetis fixed-axis projects retain incoming-normal-speed curves; switching such a project to Dou requires checking/replacing the curves, not just changing a label.

Wake & mesh tab: separate numerical resolution from physical spreading

ParameterReason and how to choose it
Refine downstream wake / length in DControls mesh refinement along the placed direction. Default length is 10 rotor diameters. Local cells are capped at D/2 or smaller if the river mesh is already finer, with 2:1 subdivision/transitions. The corridor is five diameters wide and clipped to the domain. Two cells across D is a minimum, not a convergence guarantee.
Ambient eddy viscosity (m²/s)Controls horizontal momentum mixing in the resolved water model. It affects wake recovery and flow redistribution. Choose it from appropriate turbulence/mixing evidence; it is not the same parameter as analytical wake growth k.
Power calculationResolved Thetis power uses the hydraulic field. Dou adds analytical receiver-inflow reconstruction while retaining hydraulic forces. The legacy aligned correction is available for saved-project compatibility.
Wake growth kDimensionless spreading rate in the aligned width relation σ = εD + kx. Larger k generally spreads and recovers the analytical wake more rapidly. Use independently supported wake data; BRAIDS does not derive k from the viscosity setting.
Initial width εDimensionless starting width σ/D. Together with k it sets the profile width and admissible deficit amplitude. Do not tune it solely to reproduce the downstream power being used for validation.
Minimum model spacing (D)Enforces the far-wake scope, at least 3D in this implementation. It is a validity check, not a blade diameter or refinement length. Tilted rotor integration points must also stay outside the excluded near wake.
Wake parameter sourceRecords the experiment, article or independent calibration used for k and ε. No universal wake parameters are inferred for every Swiss river.

5. Suggested workflow and outputs

  1. Prepare the river geometry, discharge/stage conditions and hydraulic roughness. Use a hydraulic preview or spin-up if you need to identify the flow direction.
  2. Place each centre and draw a downstream reference. Choose a fixed axis or self-aligning operation. Check the displayed reference and rotor-axis directions.
  3. Enter rotor geometry and clearances. Load the actual device curves. For Dou, import matching yaw-performance data and supply independent wake parameters.
  4. Prepare the mesh. Correct explicit configuration errors; do not bypass operating-envelope checks to force a run.
  5. Run and inspect water response, turbine activation, actual yaw, receiver power and detected upstream wake counts. Export turbine performance and power/energy CSV files from Results.

Useful outputs include shaft power, Ct/Cp, inferred incoming speed, local normal/tangential velocity, actual resolved yaw, force components, operating state, active upstream wakes and power-correction diagnostics. Ct/Cp may change with speed and the curve/controller convention; constant-input coefficients are deliberately constant. Power varies approximately with velocity cubed, so a seemingly small speed bias can matter.

Below the supported cut-in speed, rotors stop with zero power and force, restart when conditions recover, and produce one completion warning if this happened. Coverage, reverse-flow and curve-range rules also apply. A fixed turbine does not automatically reverse its mounting. Overlapping analytical deficits that predict nonpositive flow, source-work violations or unsupported speed/yaw remain errors.

The reported useful power is mechanical shaft power. It is not automatically electrical production: generator, drivetrain, availability and operational losses need separate justified treatment. The app's power/energy CSV capability does not supply missing efficiencies.

6. What is supported, and what remains an assumption

The Dou scalar implementation was checked against independent high-precision equations and the paper's hydrokinetic yaw-profile data. Retained aligned-array power histories and single-yaw evidence remain unchanged. CPU/GPU, hydraulic-method and restart controls cover the integration. These are different types of evidence: mathematical verification, numerical consistency and experimental comparison must not be conflated.

Experimental validation of yawed hydrokinetic receiver power in arrays is deferred. The present use in third/fourth rows is a documented prediction based on the wake model, Gaussian vertical completion, additive deficit superposition and device-response transfer. The depth-averaged model does not resolve blades, three-dimensional swirl or a fully measured vertical wake field. Current guards include receiver H/D ≥ 2, quasi-uniform ambient flow, straight unblocked wake paths and supported yaw/device ranges. Passing a numerical check does not remove those limits.

7. Placement and sediment combinations

Enter settings, then use Add turbine at the bottom of the panel. First click the centre, then click DOWNSTREAM towards the outlet, not upstream. Parameters are retained for subsequent placements.

Ordinary sediment transport is available with turbines under each sediment model's existing compatibility rules. No turbine-specific bedload-formula whitelist, MF1 restriction or static-mesh requirement is imposed. Suspension, morphology and AMR retain their own formula/integrator checks. HLLC sediment restrictions remain unchanged. The separate Jiang?Cao mixture solver has no turbine-force binding and is explicitly rejected.

The hub remains fixed in absolute elevation; current bed and surface determine clearance. Turbine force acts on water momentum, not directly as bed shear. Specify ambient viscosity only once, in either Turbines or Advanced hydrodynamics. Numerical coupled checks do not establish experimental turbine-scour validation.
